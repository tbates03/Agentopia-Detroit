# Weather, Seasons and Cultural Calendar Research Roadmap

Agentopia World League expands the right-sizing research surface beyond jobs and dialogue into environmental and calendar context.

## Weather

Participating worlds may model weather conditions that materially change the work agents perform:

- rain
- snow
- heat
- cold
- storms
- wind
- drought
- flooding
- other locally relevant conditions

Weather can affect transportation, agriculture, utilities, health, deliveries, construction, tourism, school, public events and business demand.

## Seasons

Seasonality should follow the simulated place rather than assuming Detroit or the Northern Hemisphere.

A world may represent:

- Northern Hemisphere seasons
- Southern Hemisphere seasons
- wet/dry seasonal patterns
- locally relevant agricultural or climate cycles

The purpose is to test whether right-sizing requirements change when the same task occurs under different environmental conditions.

## Holidays, observances and festivals

For each represented culture or community, a world may support **up to 50 widely celebrated holidays, observances, festivals and culturally important dates**.

This is not a universal "top 50" generated by TAi.

The calendar should be contributed or validated through:

- lived experience
- professional experience
- fluent/native-language review
- community review
- authoritative sources

A country must not automatically be treated as one culture. Multiple communities inside the same country may have different calendars.

Calendar context may affect:

- business opening and closing
- work schedules
- travel
- family gatherings
- food preparation
- purchasing
- religious activity
- school calendars
- government services
- festivals and public events
- transportation demand

## Language

Holiday and seasonal names should be preserved in the language actually used by the represented community when possible. English may be added as a maintainer summary, not as a replacement for the native-language artifact.

## Research principle

The goal is not to score cultures.

The goal is to observe whether task difficulty, model sufficiency, routing, latency or escalation changes when real environmental and cultural context is introduced.
